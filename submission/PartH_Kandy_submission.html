4. Why total revenue is a misleading comparison, and how I corrected for it

Comparing Kandy's total revenue (LKR 114,190) with Colombo's (LKR 1,224,510) suggests Kandy earns only 9% of Colombo's sales. The two totals measure very different things. Colombo's figure covers 89 trading days and Kandy's covers 7, its opening week. Colombo's period also includes the Avurudu shopping season: across the chain, 28% of all revenue fell in 1–15 April (17% of the days), and Colombo's daily revenue in that window (LKR 23,614) was double its normal level (LKR 11,761). Kandy's week has no festival. I corrected for this in three ways. First, I normalised by time, using revenue and sales per trading day. Second, for the existing branches I used only the 74 normal days, leaving out 1–15 April. Third, I cross-checked with a same-length window, the last full week of April, which has the same number of weekend days. I also used per-sale metrics (average basket, items per sale) that do not depend on period length, and compared payment mix with late April because wallet use grows monthly. After correction Kandy earns LKR 16,313 a day: 139% of Colombo and 2.9× the average branch. One limitation remains. This is a single opening week of 100 sales, and new-store novelty probably inflates it: 86 of the 100 sales came from newly registered loyalty customers, and no cost or floor-space data is available to judge profit.

5. Business recommendations

  1. Run Kandy as a top-tier store, not a small new branch. It averages 14.3 sales a day, more than Colombo (9.4) and almost three times the average branch (5.0). Weekends were busiest (LKR 19,375 a day against 15,088 on weekdays, +28%), so roster extra till staff on Saturdays and Sundays and plan stock replenishment at Colombo's level. Re-check after four weeks, because only 7 of Kandy's 93 customers returned within the first week.
  2. Build the range around beverages, especially tea. Beverages earned 30.4% of Kandy's revenue, double the chain average (15.3%). Ceylon Black Tea 400g was its best seller: 19 units in 7 days, about 2.7 a day, against 0.4 a day in a typical existing branch. Increase tea and beverage shelf space and safety stock, and run a local tea promotion to grow baskets.
  3. Bring in Colombo's electronics range with card and wallet offers. Electronics is only 8.8% of Kandy's revenue against 22.9% in Colombo, and no kettles, rice cookers or speakers were sold. Yet Kandy shoppers pay like Colombo's: 73% non-cash, against a chain average of 61%. Big-ticket electronics with card or wallet instalment deals is the clearest way to lift Kandy's average basket (LKR 1,142) towards Colombo's (LKR 1,247).
